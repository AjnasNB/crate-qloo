# Crate's contribution and competitive limits

Developed by Ajnas N B

**Same stockroom, different programme, different books. Then a stock-out without reopening the packed crate.** This is the strongest demonstrated story.

Crate combines Qloo's artist/film-to-book evidence with a joint allocation of scarce physical copies across two simultaneously departing stalls. It respects shop reserves, editorial pins/vetoes and a protected packed crate. Stock repairs minimize changed title–stall placements before improving cultural rank fit. Programme changes acquire new cultural evidence rather than reusing a generic book list.

## What is distinctive

The operational combination is the contribution. Book recommendations, inventory filtering, exact optimization and minimal-change repair are established ideas. [NoveList Select](https://about.ebsco.com/novelist/products/novelist-select) already connects recommendations with a library's collection. Crate should not claim a first-ever recommender, a new optimization algorithm or the invention of inventory-aware discovery.

The narrower workflow has an inspectable consequence: two appealing plans cannot each take the same sole copy. Preserving an already-packed crate makes last-minute repair a different task from generating two independent recommendation lists. A bookseller keeps control through explicit editorial decisions.

## Evidence and weakness

The [measured live record](LIVE-QLOO.md) establishes Qloo-dependent returned orders and assignments. The same input inventory, changed film interest and fresh query produced different ranks and selections. The Dispossessed appearing in a film/music-anchored inventory is a cross-domain connection to inspect; it is not proof of reader preference. The actual planning tool and public UI also demonstrated feasible allocation and protected-crate repair.

[A Cappella's film-book pop-up](https://www.acappellabooks.com/pages/events/1525/a-cappella-at-the-movies-a-film-book-pop-up-sale) establishes event-specific bookselling. It does not validate the frequency of simultaneous-stall conflicts or demand for Crate. Sample stock/events are fictional. No bookseller pilot, adoption, reader acceptance, time savings or sales improvement has been measured. That is the largest competitive weakness and cannot be fixed by repeating software tests or assigning a winning percentage.

## Fit to the hackathon

The [official requirements](https://qloo.devpost.com/) ask for a working Qloo-integrated application that functions as an agentic tool, uses an agent framework, or wires Qloo into an existing agent. Crate follows the agentic-tool route through its external MCP tools. A particular LLM, framework, programming language or hosting vendor is not mandated. Required submission material includes an externally published working demo, public runnable source with an open-source license, and a feature description. Video is optional.

The [official rules](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules) weight implementation, design, potential impact and idea quality equally. Crate has measured implementation evidence and a coherent bounded workflow. Potential impact is less established until real booksellers evaluate its choices and operational usefulness. Direct Cloudflare ownership improves deployment control, not proof of novelty or customer value.

## Judge walkthrough

1. Keep the stock fixed, change a film interest, confirm its real identity, and show the fresh Qloo order and changed allocation. This demonstrates Qloo's contribution.
2. Lock Soul & City, remove The Dispossessed from available stock, and repair. Show Kindred replacing it while all four protected placements remain unchanged.
3. Inspect the live snapshot and source-labeled manifest. Explain the sample-data and customer-validation limits.

Use the live application and current evidence ahead of the older synthetic motion film. Do not claim superiority to generic LLMs without a controlled comparison, or use API success as evidence that judges or readers will prefer the result.
